## Incident definition
Before discussing the incident response framework, it is crucial to establish the purpose of a security incident.

A security incident can have the resulting definitions:
- Breach of an unambiguous or implied security policy
- attempts to gain unauthorised access
- unwanted denial of resources
- unauthorised use of digital resources
- modification without the vendor's knowledge, instruction, or consent

## Incident severity level
Once the incident classifcation has occured, the severity level of the incident can be determines. 
The severity level of the incident will dictate the source of actions that should be performed to resolve the incident.

When devloping a incident response plan, efforts must be made to anticipate scenerios before they happen and to make the following decisions in advance:
- Where did the incident happen?
- Which areas of the business processes are affected?
- Who should be notified?
- What are the procedures/actions to be taken?
## Resolution action plan
When making the resolution action plan you should consider the current and future steps required to contain the incident and eradicate any threats that might exist; and the future steps necessary for restoring systems and services.

The resolution action plan should be reviewed throughout the process as it may change depending on what evidence is aquired during the detection and anaklysis steps.

#### Key Elements:
- Containment actions
- Eradication actions
- Capability and capacity requirements
- Communications actions

When developing the Resolution Action Plan consider:
- Calculating the approximate time-frame to resolve the incident
- What resources are required to determine the incident?
- What systems/services will be affected during the resolution process?
- What services are impacted?
## Data Backup
**Electronic Vaulting**
Makes copies of files as they are modified and periodically transmits them to an offiste backup.

**Tape Vaulting**
The process of moving your critical data offsite.

**Remote Journaling**
The process of recording the product of a computer application in a distant data storage environment, concurrently with the normal recording of the product in the primary environment.

**Hierachical Storage Management**
A data sotrage and data management technique that automatically moves data between high-cost and low-cost storage media

**Optical Jukebox**
A robotic data storage device that can automatically load and unload optical disks, such as Compact Disk, DVD, etc.

**Data Replication**
The act of making copies of all or specific parts of your data so that you have multiple versions of the same thing on hand if something happens with one version.
