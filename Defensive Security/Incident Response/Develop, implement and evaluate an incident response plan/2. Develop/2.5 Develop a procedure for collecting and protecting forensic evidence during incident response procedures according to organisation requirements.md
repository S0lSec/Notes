The incident response team needs to be well aware of laws, policies and procedures. Knowing these will aid in collecting evidence.

The incident response team should record the incident as soon as they suspect it. Logs must be gathered, (a logbook is a effective method). 

Events must be documented with dates and time included.
# Some key indicators to record
- Status of the incident such as new, repeated, in progress, under investigation, resolved 
- Brief incident summary 
- Incident indicators and related information 
- Other suspected threats 
- Actions by the incident response team 
- Degree of impact 
- All the evidence related to the incident 
- Observed changes and comments for the team 
- Restoration actions
## Possible evidence sources 
- Equipment such as routers, firewalls, servers, clients, portables, and embedded devices 
- Application software, such as accounting packages for evidence of fraud, ERP packages for employee records (such as identity theft), system and management archives 
- Monitoring software as Intrusion Detection Software, packet sniffers, keyboard loggers, and content checker 
- Daily logs, such as access logs, printer logs, web traffic, internal network logs, Internet traffic, database transactions, and commercial transactions 
- Other sources, such as CCTV, door access records, phone logs, PABX data, telco records, call-centre logs, and recorded messages 
- Back-ups and archives, for example, laptops and desktops

## Evidence Security
Evidence is preserved to support the organisations needs in future legal or internal proceedings.
An incident response team must follow an apparent chain of custody to avoid any allegations of altering the evidence.

The organisation should develop a policy that clearly states evidence preserving time-frames. The policy can include:
- Prosecution
- Data retention
- Cost factors

Evidence must be collected during the investigation, and they must meet these two main criteria:
1. **Acceptability of evidence** - If it can be used in any court proceedings
2. **Significance of evidence** - Completeness of evidence

Some steps that can help in preserving evidence are as follow:
- Securing and restricting access to evidence
- Change passwords and any user must be authenticated
- Unplug wireless connection or disabling the network can restrict remote attacks
## Evidence Retention
An organisation should determine how long evidence from an incident should be retained.
