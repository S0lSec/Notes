## What is an Incident?
An incident occurs when a breach of an explicit or implied digital security policy requires corrective action because it threatens the CIA of an information system or the system processes, stores or transmits.

(Put simply a incident is when the CIA of a system is threatened.)

Some examples of incidents are:
- DOS
- Malware outbreak
- Compromise or disclosure of sensitive or personal information
## How to identify an incident?
When a incident occurs it is VERY important to identify and classify it to reduce its impact immediately.

Some indicators to identify an incident are:
- Unusual activity by your security monitoring system 
- Bizarre data during audits or reporting 
- Social engineering reports peculiar events. 
- Unusual network and processing unit functioning 
- Lost access to the system 
- Malicious code or denial of service 
- Unwanted files in the system 
- Information security breach 
- Application system failure
## Reason for a incident
A incident can be caused by a variety of things, such as:
- Intentional or unintentional acts
- Actions of employees
- Action of vendors
- External or internal actions
## Incident Response Plan
An incident response plan is a systematic method of outlining the instructions to approach and manage the organisations response towards an incident.

A successful incident response plan comprises directions for specific attacks and directions on avoiding further damage by implementing recovery strategies and mitigating approaches.
## The necessity for incident response
Incident response helps in minimising the loss and disruption of services caused by any incident. 

Moreover adequate steps can put us in a confident position, gaining more insights on future happenings. Besides, it facilitates dealing with legal issues that may happen due to an incident.

Having a incident response plan ensure security and eliminates risks to the organisations databases. There are different stages in generating an efficient and structural incident response plan. 

First and foremost is requirement gathering, which plays a significant role in developing effective incident response plans. The core process of gathering requirements is to define processes, information and interactions. Requirements are categorised as functional and non-functional requirements.

Functional requirements are interactions between a system and its environment.
Non-functional requirements are both vital in identifying all the cross-connections between the human and system interface.
## Elements to be included in the incident response plan
- Scope and mission
- Tactics and goals
- Approval from management and stakeholders
- Approaches to confront known threats
- Metrics to measure the response effectiveness
- Roadmap to incident response capability 
- Regular assessment to check the validity of the IR plan towards organisations requirements
## Actions to gather requirements
1. Determine the scale of your incident response plan 
2. Developing and reviewing the security policies of your organisation 
3. Identifying the organisational assets 
4. Defining the key stakeholders 
5. Assigning roles and responsibilities 
6. Generating a communication plan 
7. Identifying the required tools and equipment's 
8. Organisations provide adequate training to the incident response team and staff

Use adequate elicitation methods such as:
- Brainstorming
- Focus groups
- Prototyping
- Interviews
- etc

When identifying the needs, ask questions with "what", "who", "when" and "how" to aid in discovering vulnerabilities in the security systems.
(Example: As an instance, "What are the key assets?" "Who has access to confidential documents?" "When will the security fails?" "Where would the data be accessed?" "What must be done?")
## The incident response plan categorisation
- Pre-incident response plan
- During the incident response plan
- Post-incident response plan
## Documentation
Documentation must be organised to provide a roadmap for generating a successful incident response plan.

The incident response plan will act as a guideline for management and relevant stakeholders. This, assist in making final decisions and ensure its allegiance to the organisation structure.

The documentation can be used as legal evidence in court if an incident is considered a criminal act. 
## Documents required
- Policy and procedure document 
- Assigning roles and responsibilities plan (Incident management team) 
- Executives and management team 
- Classification of the incident and possible triggers 
- Develop incident response actions and activities 
- Communication engagement plan 
- Document and Keep the records of Response of Activities
## Tips for creating compelling documents are:
- Use of explicit language avoiding jargon 
- Validate the documentation and follow the review and feedback process 
- Including visuals as texts, flowcharts, use case diagrams, and graphs
