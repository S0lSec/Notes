## 1. Identify potential emergencies
- Identify various emergencies that could potentially affect your infrastructure
- Should be based on the risk assessment
## 2. Create response plan
- For each emergency develop a response plan
- Include immediate actions to be taken, ensuring safety and minimised damage
	- Evacuation plans
	- First aid procedures
	- Immediate system shutdowns
## 3. Assign responsibilities
- Clearly define who is response for executing each part of the plan

**Incident Commander**
- Person in charge during an emergency situation
- They coordinate and control all incident-related actives
**Safety Officer**
- Ensures all actions taken during the emergency response adhere to safety standards and procedures
**Public Information Officer (PIO)**
- Communicates with the media and the public during an emergency situation, sharing accurate and timely information
**Liaison Officer**
- Coordinates with outside agencies and partners. Such as law enforcement, utility companies or government agencies
**Operations Section Chief**
- Oversees all actions related to managing the immediate effects of the emergency
**Planning Section Chief**
- Gathers and evaluates information about the emergency, and developing strategies and plans to manage it
**Logistics Section Chief**
- Ensures all necessary resources are available:
	- Personnel
	- Equipment
	- Supplies
**Finance/Administration Section Chief**
- Oversees financial and administrative aspects
- Such as:
	- Tracking costs
	- Handling claims
	- Managing procurement during the emergency
## 4. Develop recovery plans
- Once the immediate response has been handles, recovery plans detail how to restore normal operations.
	- System repairs
	- Data recovery
	- Re-establishment of physical security
## 5. Conduct drills
- Test the plans by conducting emergency drills.
## 6. Review and update plans
- Review results of each drill and use them to improve the plans.
- Update the plans as your organisation, infrastructure or risk profile changes
