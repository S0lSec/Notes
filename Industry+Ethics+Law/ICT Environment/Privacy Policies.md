# Key Concepts
- **Compliance Benchmarking:** Comparing policy content and procedures to current legal and industry standards
- **Policy-Practice Alignment:** Checking if documented privacy rules match actual organisational behavior.
- **Transparency:** Clear explanations of how personal information is collected, used and protected.
# Current regulatory actions
- New privacy enforcement powers introduced in 2024 reforms
- 2026 marks the first national OAIC privacy compliance sweep
- Organisations face penalties for outdated or incomplete policies
- Transparency requirements have increased, especially for data use
- Reviews must reflect current legal and regulatory expectations
# Why compliance reviews matter
- OAIC now focuses on enforcing, not advising privacy obligations
- Non-compliant policies attract infringement notices and penalties
- Public-facing privacy policies are reviewed against actual practice
- Strong compliance reduces reputational and regulatory risk
-  A review is no longer optional; it is a compliance safeguard.
# What a privacy policy must contain
- How personal data is collected, stored and disclosed
- The purpose for collecting personal information
- Access, correction and compliant process
- Security controls protecting personal information
- Missing required details may now trigger regulatory action
# Reviewing Procedures
- Examine how staff collect information
- Confirm procedures align with written policy
- Identify undocumented practices or inconsistencies
- Validate secure handling and storage steps
# Assessing data handling practices
- Verify secure storage and encryption methods
- Review consent processes during in-person data collection
- Check accuracy and correction workflows
- Ensure retention and deletion processes are documented
# Identifying compliance gaps
- Outdated references to legislation
- Missing breach-notifications processes
- No mention of third-party data sharing
- Retention schedules do not align to procedures
# Evidence-based review methods
- Compare policy wording with actual system behavior
- Analyse logs, workflows and access reports
- Consult system administrators and data owners
- Review previous audits and incident reports
# Signs a policy needs updating
- New software or data-collection systems implemented
- Structural or staffing changes in the organisation
- Regulatory bodies have issued updated requirements
- Inconsistent practices found through audits
