### 1. [Privacy Act 1988 ](https://www.cyber.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/PROTECT%20-%20Essential%20Eight%20Maturity%20Model%20%28November%202022%29.pdf)(Cth)
- Sets out the [Australian Privacy Principles (APPs)](https://www.oaic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/1125/app-guidelines-july-2019.pdf), which govern how organisations collect, use, store, and disclose personal information
- APP 11 specifically requires "reasonable steps" to protect personal information from misuse, loss, and unauthorised access
- Applies to most businesses with turnover over $3 million, plus some smaller businesses (health providers, etc.)
- Breaches can trigger obligations under the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme (below)

### 2. Notifiable Data Breaches (NDB) Scheme
- Part of the Privacy Act; mandates notification to the OAIC and affected individuals when a data breach is likely to result in serious harm
- Notification must occur "as soon as practicable"
- Non-compliance can result in penalties from the OAIC
- Directly relevant to email/M365 environments since email is a common breach vector

### 3. Security of Critical Infrastructure Act 2018 (SOCI Act)
- Applies to organisations operating "critical infrastructure" (energy, water, healthcare, communications, financial services, food/grocery, etc.)
- Requires mandatory cyber incident reporting to the Australian Cyber Security Centre (ACSC)
- Introduces obligations for Critical Infrastructure Risk Management Programs (CIRMP)
- Government has "step-in" powers to direct entities during major cyber incidents

### 4. Cyber Security Act 2024 (Cth)
- Australia's first standalone cyber security law, introduced following major breaches (Optus, Medibank)
- Introduces mandatory ransomware payment reporting for certain businesses
- Establishes a Cyber Incident Review Board to review major incidents
- Sets mandatory security standards for smart/IoT devices

### 5. Spam Act 2003
- Regulates unsolicited commercial electronic messages (email, SMS)
- Requires consent, sender identification, and functional unsubscribe mechanisms
- Enforced by the Australian Communications and Media Authority (ACMA)
- Relevant to marketing emails sent via platforms like M365/Exchange

### 6. Telecommunications Act 1997 & Telecommunications (Interception and Access) Act 1979
- Governs security obligations for telecommunications carriers and carriage service providers
- Includes the Telecommunications Sector Security Reforms (TSSR), requiring providers to protect networks from unauthorised interference
- Relevant if the organisation is a telco or ISP

### 7. Corporations Act 2001 (Cth)
- Directors' duties (s180) can extend to cyber security oversight — failure to manage cyber risk may constitute a breach of duty of care and diligence
- ASIC has pursued enforcement action (e.g., against RI Advice) for inadequate cyber risk management
- Relevant to board-level cyber governance obligations

### 8. Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA) — CPS 234
- Applies to APRA-regulated entities (banks, insurers, superannuation funds)
- Requires information security capability commensurate with vulnerabilities and threats
- Mandates incident notification to APRA within 72 hours of a material breach
- Requires third-party/vendor security assessments (relevant to cloud services like M365)

### 9. My Health Records Act 2012
- Governs security and privacy of the My Health Record system
- Imposes specific penalties for unauthorised collection, use, or disclosure of health information
- Relevant to healthcare organisations using email/digital systems to handle patient data

### 10. State-Based Records/Archives Legislation
- E.g., State Records Act 1998 (NSW), Public Records Act 1973 (Vic)
- Governs retention, security, and disposal of government records, including electronic communications
- Relevant to public sector organisations

### 11. Criminal Code Act 1995 (Cth) — Part 10.7
- Contains the core Commonwealth computer offences: unauthorised access, modification, or impairment of data/electronic communications
- Covers hacking, creating/distributing malware, denial-of-service attacks, and unauthorised access to restricted data
- Provides the criminal law basis for prosecuting cyber attackers in Australia
- Relevant because it underpins law enforcement response when Acme is a _victim_ of a cyber incident

### 12. Cybercrime Act 2001 (Cth)
- Amended the Criminal Code to modernise computer offences for the digital era
- Gives law enforcement powers to search/seize electronic data under warrant
- Complements Part 10.7 of the Criminal Code
